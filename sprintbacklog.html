What is the difference between a Sprint Backlog and a Product Backlog?

The Product Backlog is an organized, evolving list of work needed to improve or develop a product. It includes features, improvements, bug fixes, and other requirements that may need to be completed. The Product Owner is responsible for managing and prioritizing the Product Backlog to ensure the team focuses on delivering value. As project needs change, items can be added, removed, or adjusted.

The Sprint Backlog is a smaller, more focused plan that identifies the work the Developers select for a specific Sprint. During Sprint Planning, the team establishes a Sprint Goal and determines which Product Backlog Items (PBIs) can realistically be completed. The Sprint Backlog includes the Sprint Goal, selected PBIs, and a plan for completing the work. Developers continuously update this plan throughout the Sprint as they learn more and make progress toward the Sprint Goal.

The primary difference between the two backlogs is their scope and purpose. The Product Backlog represents the broader work needed for the product, while the Sprint Backlog focuses on the team's immediate objectives. For example, a team developing a website might have a Product Backlog containing new webpages, navigation improvements, and accessibility features. During a Sprint, the Developers might select two webpages and a navigation update to complete. Those selected items become part of the Sprint Backlog, while the remaining work stays in the Product Backlog for future consideration.
