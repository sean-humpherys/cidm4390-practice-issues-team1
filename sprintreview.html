What is a Sprint Review activity?

The Sprint Review is a Scrum event held near the end of each Sprint to inspect the outcome of the Sprint and determine future adaptations. During this event, the Scrum Team presents the results of its work to key stakeholders and discusses progress toward the Product Goal. The Sprint Review provides an opportunity to evaluate what was accomplished, gather feedback, and consider changes in the product or business environment. It is a collaborative working session rather than simply a presentation or demonstration.

The Sprint Review involves the Scrum Team, including the Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Developers, along with key stakeholders. The Product Owner discusses progress toward the Product Goal and may explain how current priorities have changed. Developers share the completed work and answer questions about the product. Stakeholders provide feedback, suggest improvements, and help the team understand emerging needs. For example, during a Sprint Review for a Scrum educational website, the team might demonstrate completed webpages about Daily Scrum and Product Backlog Grooming. Stakeholders could suggest clearer navigation or additional examples to make the website easier to use.

The Sprint Review helps the Scrum Team inspect its progress and adapt the Product Backlog based on what has been learned. It is not intended to be a formal approval meeting, and completed work does not need to wait for the Sprint Review to be released. The Product Owner may adjust the Product Backlog to reflect new opportunities, stakeholder feedback, or changing priorities. The event is timeboxed to a maximum of four hours for a one-month Sprint, with shorter Sprints generally requiring shorter reviews. By encouraging transparency, collaboration, and continuous improvement, the Sprint Review helps ensure the team delivers valuable outcomes.
